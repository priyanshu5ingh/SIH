# Identifier Strategy for ULPIN System

## OFFICIAL ULPIN

The Official Unique Land Parcel Identification Number (ULPIN), also known as Bhu-Aadhaar, is a 14-digit alphanumeric identifier based on the latitude and longitude coordinates of a land parcel. It is generated using the parcel's interior point coordinates and complies with international standards such as ECCMA/PNIU and OGC. The ULPIN serves as a "Single, Authoritative Source of Truth" for land parcels under the Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP) administered by the Department of Land Resources (DoLR), Government of India. It has been rolled out in 29 states/UTs with pilots in four others.

## PROPOSED VERTICAL SPATIAL IDENTIFIER / VPID

The term "Vertical Spatial Identifier" (or VPID, inferred from Vertical Property Mapping System) is not an official government-defined term. In the context of the Smart India Hackathon 2026 problem statement (SIH26011) and related projects, it refers to the proposed extension of ULPIN to the vertical dimension for multi-story buildings (apartments, basements, utility corridors, etc.). This extension creates a 3D ULPIN by appending additional codes (such as floor level, unit number, etc.) to the base 14‑digit ULPIN to uniquely identify each vertical subunit. The geo‑identity‑stack project explicitly states that the 3D identifier shown is a "proposed 3D ULPIN extension, not an officially adopted Government of India format."

## INTERNAL DATABASE ID

The Internal Database ID refers to the primary key or surrogate key used within the state‑level land records IT systems (such as the NLRMP/DILRMP databases) to uniquely identify a land parcel record. This identifier is typically a numeric or UUID value generated by the database and is not meant for public consumption. Examples include the internal record ID in the bhulekh database, the survey number, khasra number, or khatoni number when used as a key in the computerized system. While the ULPIN is designed to be a standardized, interoperable identifier across states and agencies, the Internal Database ID is specific to the implementation of each state's land records software.

**Sources:**
- General knowledge of land record computerisation under DILRMP; no specific government document defines this term, but it is evident from the architecture of land record databases.

## 3D GEOMETRY ID

The 3D Geometry ID is not a formally defined term in government documentation. In GIS and 3D mapping contexts, it can refer to a hash or identifier derived from the three‑dimensional geometry (volume) of a property—for example, a spatial hash of the 3D polygon or mesh representing a building’s footprint and height. Such an identifier may be used internally for spatial indexing, change detection, or linking 3D models to land records. It is distinct from both the ULPIN (which is coordinate‑based) and any database key.

**Sources:**
- Inferred from GIS and 3D‑city‑model practices; no specific government source defines this term for land parcels.

## RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE IDENTIFIERS

1. **Official ULPIN** is the foundational 2D identifier for every land parcel, based solely on its geographic footprint (latitude/longitude). It is the standard promoted by the Government of India.

2. **Proposed Vertical Spatial Identifier (VPID)** extends the Official ULPIN to the third dimension. A complete 3D ULPIN would consist of the base 14‑digit ULPIN plus additional characters representing vertical levels (e.g., floor, subunit). This extension is still proposals or prototypes (as seen in SIH26011 and geo‑identity‑stack) and has not been adopted as a national standard.

3. **Internal Database ID** is used internally by each state's land records IT system to manage parcel records. It is typically mapped to the Official ULPIN (or vice versa) for integration and reporting purposes, but remains an implementation‑specific key.

4. **3D Geometry ID** may be used in GIS/3D‑modeling pipelines to represent the spatial extent of a 3D property. It can be linked to the 3D ULPIN (if adopted) or to the Official ULPIN for visualization and analysis, but it serves a different purpose (geometric identification vs. alphanumeric coding).

In summary, the Official ULPIN is the government‑standardized 2D identifier. The other identifiers represent internal, proposed, or geometric extensions that serve specific technical or operational needs but are not officially recognized as part of the ULPIN standard unless formally adopted by the Department of Land Resources.
